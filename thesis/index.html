What if learning data could do more than tell us who logged in?

A research-led model for connecting the dots between learning activity, employee capability and business performance — then turning those insights into a useful next step.

By Tolga Gündal · MSc Data Analytics · Berlin

A completed master’s research project combining qualitative expert interviews, thematic analysis and the development of a four-layer Learning Analytics Model.

The question behind the research

Companies have learning data. But can they connect it to what changes at work?

LMS and LXP platforms record completions, logins and time spent. Useful signals — but they don't automatically tell an L&D team whether someone has become more capable, whether performance has improved, or what to do next.

01 / Fragmented

Data lives in separate systems

Learning, HR and business data often sit in different tools, use inconsistent identifiers and are difficult to bring together.

02 / Misleading

Activity is not the same as impact

A completed course or a long session can look positive in a report without demonstrating skill transfer or business value.

03 / Hard to interpret

Dashboards can create more work

Complex charts and raw metrics still leave busy L&D professionals with the task of deciding what action to take.

04 / Disconnected

Insight doesn't always lead to action

Without a clear link to business priorities, analytics risks becoming another reporting exercise rather than a decision-support capability.

The useful question isn't only “What happened in the LMS?” It's “What changed at work — and what should we do next?”
Stop treating activity as impact

Completion and login rates are operational signals, not proof of learning transfer or ROI.

Connect learning to work

Look for relationships between learning and indicators such as time-to-proficiency, error reduction and sales performance.

Make the next step clear

Professionals need guidance they can act on, not another dashboard that leaves interpretation entirely to them.

The central contribution

The Learning Analytics Model (LAM)

The model developed from the interview findings is designed as a strategic navigation system — not simply a reporting dashboard. Select each layer to see how it moves from disconnected data to a practical intervention.

Layer 01 · Foundation

Integration

Bring fragmented learning and workforce information into a consistent, reliable view. The proposed layer uses ETL logic — extract, transform and load — to clean and merge data from systems such as LMS, HRIS and CRM.

  • Resolve inconsistent identifiers and formats.
  • Reduce manual data preparation.
  • Create a dependable foundation for analysis.
LMS / LXP+HRIS+CRM→Clean, connected data
The four-layer architecture is the proposed conceptual model derived from the research. It is not presented as a deployed software product or a validated production system.
From measurement to management

What could this change for an L&D team?

From course completion → skill transfer

Pair learning records with role-specific performance signals to ask whether capability is showing up in day-to-day work.

From static reports → targeted support

Identify teams or skill areas that may need a different learning intervention, rather than sending the same content to everyone.

From activity counts → business context

Explore links between learning and relevant KPIs — for example, time-to-proficiency, error rates, sales or customer outcomes.

From “what might happen?” → “what should we do?”

Use prescriptive or hybrid logic to recommend a next step, while keeping people accountable for decisions and context.

One practitioner example discussed in the research illustrated time-to-proficiency: reducing onboarding from three months to two. This was an interview example used to explain the metric — not an outcome measured or delivered by this study.

How the research was done

Built by listening to people doing the work.

This was a qualitative, exploratory study. I used semi-structured interviews with nine senior professionals in L&D, EdTech and People Analytics, then applied thematic analysis to identify recurring barriers, metric preferences and model requirements.

01 · Listen

Capture practical experiences from professionals working with corporate learning and people data.

02 · Identify patterns

Compare recurring themes around data integration, measurement, data literacy and decision-making.

03 · Design the model

Map the findings into four connected layers: Integration, Strategic Value, Analytical and Action.

Scope matters: nine expert interviews provide qualitative insight and a basis for a proposed framework. The study does not claim statistical representativeness, causal proof of ROI, or empirical validation through a live enterprise deployment. Implementation and testing in a real corporate environment would be the next step.
